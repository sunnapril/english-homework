2 · Punctuation after opening words and phrases

Comma or no comma?

Usually add a comma after:
• opening linkers: However, Therefore, For example,
• comments on the whole sentence: With hindsight, To be honest, Generally speaking,
• an opening dependent clause: If…, Although…, Before…

Usually no comma: after a short time or place expression such as Last year or At home, unless the writer wants a special pause.
1. With hindsight ___ I would have tested the cheaper plan first.
The opening phrase comments on a past decision.
2. However ___ the default layout is already coherent.
An opening linker is normally followed by a comma.
3. For example ___ the studio could offer a shorter trial period.
This phrase introduces an example.
4. To be honest ___ I did not understand the first explanation.
This phrase comments on the speaker’s attitude.
5. If the trial period ends tomorrow ___ we need to decide today.
Use a comma after an opening dependent clause.
6. Although the instructions were lengthy ___ they were coherent.
Use a comma after an opening contrast clause.
7. Last year ___ the company extended the warranty.
A short time expression normally needs no comma here.
8. At home ___ I use a much older monitor.
A short place expression normally needs no comma here.

Find the accurate relative clause

1. A That is the artist who exhibition has just opened.
B That is the artist whose exhibition has just opened.
Use whose for possession.
2. A My father, who now has a studio in New York, is a well-known painter.
B My father who now has a studio in New York is a well-known painter.
The clause adds extra information about an already identified person.
3. A The materials I pay more for are always better quality.
B The materials for that I pay more are always better quality.
The preposition may stay at the end in a defining clause.
4. A The main character dies at the end of the film, which made me cry.
B The main character dies at the end of the film which made me cry.
The whole preceding event caused the reaction.
5. A My workshop is in London, that means travelling by train every day.
B My workshop is in London, which means travelling by train every day.
Use which, not that, after the comma.
6. A The customer for whom Jim made the sculpture did not like it.
B The customer for who Jim made the sculpture did not like it.
After a preposition, use whom for a person in formal English.

Four ways to say “make longer”

Correction from the previous lesson: extend the time available is more natural than lengthen the time available. Use extend with a deadline, opening hours, a service or available time; use lengthen with a process, speech, skirt or other measurable length.
