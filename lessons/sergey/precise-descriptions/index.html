Combine, do not copy

Turn two facts into one natural sentence

Write one sentence. Decide whether the second fact identifies the noun or merely adds extra information.

1. I returned the laptop stand. Its base had started to bend after only two days.
I returned the laptop stand whose base had started to bend after only two days.
Whose is not limited to people. It can connect a thing, organisation or place with something that belongs to it or is one of its parts. Here, it connects the laptop stand with its base. We cannot replace whose directly with which or that.
2. My spare phone still works reliably. It is nearly eight years old.
Option 1: My spare phone, which still works reliably, is nearly eight years old.
Main message: the phone is nearly eight years old.

Option 2: My spare phone, which is nearly eight years old, still works reliably.
Main message: the phone still works reliably. Both versions are correct; the information inside the relative clause is presented as secondary.
3. We visited a small workshop. The cases are assembled there by hand.
We visited the small workshop where the cases are assembled by hand. / ... in which the cases are assembled by hand.
4. The technician recommended a repair service. I had never heard of it before.
The technician recommended a repair service I had never heard of before.

Show a model after Sergey has decided

I would choose the TravelPro, which is light enough to carry but sturdy enough for Lena’s laptop. The FoldFlat is cheaper, but its flimsy joints and 1.5 kg limit make it risky. The SteelBase would be reliable, but its weight and size are not practical for somebody who walks to cafés. Taking her budget, bag space and laptop weight into account, the TravelPro offers the optimal balance.
