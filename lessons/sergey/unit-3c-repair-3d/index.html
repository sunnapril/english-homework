Clear choices, polite complaints

Repair the difficult points from Unit 3C, then start Unit 3D.

1 · Direct answers

Neither, either or both?

neither + singular noun = not the first one and not the second one
either + singular noun = one of the two; after a negative verb: not the first one and not the second one
both + plural noun = the two together

Why singular? Neither and either look at the two people or things separately: neither one / either one. The noun stays singular: neither side, either option. Use both sides when you treat the two sides together.
1. ___ side was completely right.
The meaning is: not one side and not the other.
2. I couldn't fully agree with ___ side.
After a negative verb, use either.
3. ___ people had a reasonable point.
The noun is plural and the statement includes the two people.
Talk it through: A designer wants speed; a developer wants more testing. Are both partly right, or is neither solution workable? Give one direct answer and one reason.
2 · Precision pairs

Intense or intensive?

intense = very strong in degree: intense pressure, pain, heat, rivalry.
intensive = concentrated work in a limited period: an intensive course, training, research, care.
1. The companies are involved in ___ rivalry.
Rivalry is strong, not a course of concentrated study.
2. She completed an ___ two-week training programme.
The work is concentrated into two weeks.

3 · Word building repaired

Form and meaning are separate decisions

Do not add a suffix mechanically. First recognise the word family, then check meaning and spelling.
sense → sensible = reasonable; sensitive = easily affected / needing careful treatment
1. Changing the deadline was a ___ decision.
The decision was reasonable.
2. Salary can be a ___ issue at work.
The topic needs careful treatment.
Quick contrast: Give one example of a sensible way to discuss a sensitive issue.

5 · Spoken English

What does do mean?

It'll do. = it is good enough for this purpose.
That should do. = that should be enough.
Will Monday do? = is Monday suitable?
I could do with a break. = I would really like/need a break.
1. “Will 6 p.m. do?” means…
The speaker is checking whether the time works.
2. “The first draft isn't perfect, but it'll do.” means…
The draft meets the minimum need.
Mini-dialogues: a temporary solution is acceptable; three examples are enough; Monday is suitable; you need a break.
6 · Unit 3D · SB p.28

How to make a polite complaint

This is the first guided introduction. You do not have to invent the language before seeing a model.

Three-part model
1 · Get attention politely: Excuse me. / Sorry, could I ask you something?
2 · State the problem: I asked for chips, but I’ve got mashed potato.
3 · Ask for action: Could you change it, please? / Could you check on our order for me?
1 · Cold soup
The first option checks the problem without directly blaming the waiter.
2 · Wrong side dish
Describe exactly what you ordered and what you received.
3 · A long wait
Use Could you + base verb for a polite request.
Supported dialogue: choose one problem, read or adapt one model sentence, then add one request beginning with Could you…?
