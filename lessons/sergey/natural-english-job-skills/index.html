1 · Feedback that helps

A strong answer, made more natural

Sergey’s open answer had a clear timeline, specific examples and a convincing conclusion. Improve the wording without changing the ideas.

1. After two years, I decided to change my computer because it had become too slow.
replace my computer — get a different one because the old one is no longer suitable. Exchange usually means returning an item to a shop or swapping things with another person.
2. The first model had too much limitations for the work I needed to do.
too many limitations — limitation is a countable noun here.
3. In one moment, I considered buying an e-reader instead.
At one point means “at a particular stage in a longer period”. For a moment describes a very short duration.
4. It did not please my expectations at first.
meet my expectations is the natural collocation.
5. I originally expected the tablet to replace my laptop, but now I use it for only three clearly defined purposes.
I now expect less from it and use it for fewer, more specific tasks. Fewer compares the number of countable tasks with the larger number expected before. More specific describes their narrower, clearly defined purpose. The two comparatives refer to different qualities, so they work together.
2 · Real speech repair

Keep the idea; choose the natural form

Choose the sentence you would use. Then say one true sentence about your own habits.

A I was too much sleepy that day.
B I was too sleepy that day.
B. Too + adjective: too sleepy. Compare: I slept too much.
A I was surprised every time I checked my statistics.
B I was wondered every time I see my statistics.
A. The feeling is surprised; keep the past time reference consistent: checked.
A The app recommended me too much videos.
B The app recommended too many videos to me.
B. Recommend something to somebody; use many with countable videos.
A It surprised me a lot.
B It was very surprised me.
A. A thing surprises a person; a person is surprised.
A It happened many times.
B It happened much time moments.
A. Use many times for repeated occasions; a lot of time means a long duration.
3 · A short return

Three meanings from Monday

The fuller explanation is in the lesson PDF. Here, choose only by the intended meaning.

1. The premium plan is unlikely to ___ students who only need basic storage.
The plan seems attractive to a group of people.
appeal to
2. The report contains accurate figures, but the layout is so confusing that it is not a ___ summary.
clear — easy to understand and well organised.
3. The consultant wants to name the people who should test the new system: “I recommend ___.”
that the support team test it first explicitly identifies who should act. Testing it first is grammatical but leaves the actor general.

5 · Retrieve → distinguish

Which requirement matters most?

Use the bank immediately below. Click a phrase after you have used it, so it becomes visibly crossed out.

1. Emergency engineer: Equipment sometimes fails in remote locations, so the engineer must remain calm and find a practical solution quickly.
2. International reporter: The reporter interviews people abroad and may need to challenge an official explanation politely.
challenge an explanation = question whether it is accurate or complete; it does not necessarily mean attacking the speaker.
3. Game designer: The designer turns a basic plot into scenes that players can understand without lengthy instructions.
lengthy = longer than is necessary or convenient. Long instructions is possible but neutral; lengthy adds mild criticism.
1 strong problem-solving skills and the ability to cope with pressure. 2 be fluent in a foreign language and be willing to question explanations. 3 have a flair for visual storytelling.

6 · Grammar map

Obligation, no obligation or prohibition?

MeaningPresentPast
necessarymust / have to / need to / am-is-are required tohad to / needed to / was-were required to
not necessary; there is a choicedon’t have to / don’t need to / needn’t / am-is-are not required todidn’t have to / didn’t need to / wasn’t-weren’t required to
against the rulesmustn’t / can’t / am-is-are not allowed towasn’t-weren’t allowed to
Agreement: one candidate was required to; all candidates were required to. The negative wasn’t/weren’t required to means there was no obligation. It does not mean prohibition.
Important: You don’t have to attend means attendance is optional. You mustn’t attend means attendance is prohibited.
Classify: O (obligation), NO (no obligation) or P (prohibition).

1. Visitors must wear a security badge.

2. Remote employees do not have to come in on Fridays.

3. Applicants are not allowed to share confidential work samples.

4. Last year, interns had to attend every training session.

5. Before the policy changed, staff did not need to record short internal calls.

Ask: Is it necessary, optional, or against the rules?
1 O · 2 NO · 3 P · 4 O · 5 NO
7 · Controlled practice

Choose the meaning, then build the form

1. The laboratory contains dangerous chemicals. Visitors touch any equipment. (prohibition)
Use a present form meaning “against the rules”.
mustn’t / aren’t allowed to
2. The interview is online, so applicants travel to the company’s office. (no obligation)
The journey is optional, not forbidden.
don’t have to / don’t need to / aren’t required to
3. When Lara started the job in 2018, she complete a three-month training course. (past obligation)
The course was necessary in the past.
had to / was required to / needed to. Needed to is possible when the necessity came from the situation; was required to sounds like a formal rule.
4. Freelance designers choose their own software; they use the company’s preferred package. (no obligation)
They may use it, but the company does not insist.
don’t have to / don’t need to / aren’t required to
5. All candidates provide evidence of relevant experience before the final interview. (formal requirement)
Use the passive form often found in official rules. The subject is plural.
are required to is the targeted formal wording. Must and have to are also grammatically correct; they are simply less explicitly formal here.
6. At my previous company, we install unauthorised software on work laptops. (past prohibition)
The rule prohibited the action in the past; it was not merely optional.
weren’t allowed to
