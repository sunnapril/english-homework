Whose idea is it?

Possessive adjectives and pronouns in complete situations. Everything needed is on these slides.

Block 1 · 7 minutes

Make the meaning precise

1. When I reviewed your revised plan yesterday, some parts of it unclear to me. (past form of be)
Yesterday makes the past time clear. The subject is the plural noun parts.
2. Could you revise those parts and make clearer? (object pronoun)
Replace those parts with a plural object pronoun.
3. The testers found faults in several connected components, so they should check again before releasing the update. (three-word noun phrase)
They must examine all parts together, not simply check how one component is progressing.
4. If the team postponed the release by one week, they could finish the work without being under . (noun phrase)
Use so much + uncountable noun, or such + adjective + noun.

Block 3 · 8 minutes

Complete workplace situations

1. “Is this your copy of the contract?” — “No, ___ is on my desk.”
The blank replaces my copy; no noun follows.
2. Elena presented ___ solution first, and then the team presented theirs.
The noun solution follows the blank.
3. Their deadline is Friday, but ___ is not until Wednesday.
The blank replaces our deadline.
4. The company postponed ___ product launch after a security test failed.
You need possession before a noun, not it is.
Block 4 · 8 minutes

Avoid repeating the noun

Model: My plan is cheaper than your plan. → My plan is cheaper than yours.
1. Their office is smaller than our office.
Replace our office with one word.
2. I checked my figures, but Leo has not checked his figures yet.
His can stand independently.
Show models

Their office is smaller than ours.

I checked my figures, but Leo has not checked his yet.

Block 5 · 7 minutes

Ask and answer without repeating

Whose laptop is this? — It’s mine.
Whose notes are these? — They’re Daniel’s.
1. You find an umbrella beside Marta’s chair. Ask who owns it.
2. Leon says it belongs to Marta. Answer without repeating umbrella.
Show models

Whose umbrella is this?

It’s hers. / It’s Marta’s.

Block 6 · 6 minutes

One apostrophe changes the meaning

1. The application saves ___ settings automatically.
The settings belong to the application.
2. We can release the update because ___ passed every test.
Expand it as it has passed.
Block 7 · 7 minutes

Make the owner clear

1. I sent my section, but Nina is still editing .
Replace her section.
2. Their estimate covers design, while estimate includes testing.
A noun follows the blank.
3. Our proposal reduces cost, but reduces delivery time.
Replace their proposal.
Block 8 · 8 minutes

Replace repeated nouns with the right possessive form

Documents on the shared desk
you → blue folder · Maya → red folder · our team → green folder · another team → black folder
1. “Is the blue folder Maya’s?” — “No, it’s .” (belongs to me)
No noun follows the blank.
2. My folder is blue, while Maya’s is red. The red one is .
Replace her folder.
3. The green folder belongs to our team, so it is .
Replace our team’s folder.
4. The black folder belongs to the other team, so it is .
Replace their folder.
Block 9 · only if there is time

Choose one question and answer in 3–4 complete sentences

A. A colleague accidentally takes your charger after a meeting. How would you explain politely that it belongs to you, not to them?
B. Two teams have suggested different solutions to the same problem. How would you compare your team’s idea with theirs without repeating the word idea each time?

Do only one question. The aim is to use possessive forms naturally, not to include every form.
