Your answer, polishedThe ultimate example of games copying each other is the games published by Sony. Sony has a lot of PlayStation exclusives, and while PlayStation 4 was the most successful console in their history, they found the ultimate formula for their games: a third-person action game with the same stealth mechanics, the same type of menus and the same progression through colourful items that the character can equip. If the game is open or semi-open world, it has towers or bases that you have to clear to unlock part of the map, collectibles all over the map, and side missions with secondary mechanics that appear in the main story only once. Following this formula, they released The Last of Us Part I and II, three Spider-Man games, two Horizon games, Days Gone and, not so long ago, a Wolverine game. Of course, gamers started making jokes about Sony-published games, even though other aspects of them were good.

To balance it out, I want to talk about one game that was unlike anything before or since. It was published by Sony too, but it wasn't made by an in-house studio: Death Stranding. From the first teaser right up to the release, nobody could imagine what type of game it would be, and after the demos nobody could believe it was a game only about deliveries, but it is. Of course, it has common mechanics in the stealth and shooting segments, but most of the time you deliver different types of cargo, and it was a surprisingly good game with a great story and a wonderful atmosphere.
Also in your text: collection items → collectibles · open part of the map → unlock · which you should clear → which you have to clear (it's a rule of the game, not advice) · only one time → only once · even if → even though (it really was true) · most of time → most of the time.
