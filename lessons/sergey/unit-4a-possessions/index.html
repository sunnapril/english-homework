Meaning before grammar

Which possession would you keep?

Three people must move into much smaller homes. Decide which one object each person should keep and explain why.

Daniel: an elegant mechanical watch from his grandfather; a new designer speaker; twelve ordinary coffee mugs.

Aisha: a priceless family photograph; a massive decorative mirror; a flimsy folding chair.

Viktor: a sturdy wooden toolbox he uses every week; a sparkly souvenir; an identical spare phone.

Show possible reasoning after discussion

Daniel should keep the mechanical watch because it has a family story. Aisha should keep the photograph, which is irreplaceable. Viktor should keep the sturdy toolbox that he still uses regularly.

Two older phrases: We often get attached to objects that carry memories. A name or phrase has a nice ring to it when it sounds attractive or memorable.

Grammar · relative clauses

Identify the thing or add extra information?

Defining: identifies exactly which person or thing we mean; no commas.
Non-defining: adds extra information about something already identified; use commas and do not use that.
1. The laptop that has a cracked screen belongs to me. Is the relative clause defining or non-defining?
There is more than one laptop, so the clause identifies the correct one.
2. My first guitar, which I bought when I was sixteen, is still in my parents' flat.
“My first guitar” already identifies the object; the clause adds a detail.
3. I returned the webcam cover did not fit my laptop.
The object is a thing and the pronoun is the subject of “did not fit”.
4. The technician repaired my computer also explained how to prevent the problem.
Use the subject relative pronoun for a person.
5. The watch, still keeps perfect time, was made in 1972.
This is extra information between commas; “that” is not possible.
Why not that? The commas show that the watch is already identified and the clause only adds extra information. In a non-defining relative clause, use which, not that. Without commas, both are possible: The watch that/which still keeps perfect time is in the drawer.
Grammar control

Can the relative pronoun disappear?

You can omit who/which/that when it is the object of the relative clause: The book (that) I borrowed…
You cannot omit it when it is the subject: The book that explains the system…
1. The headphones that I bought last month have already stopped working.
I bought the headphones: “that” receives the action.
2. The headphones that block outside noise are in my desk drawer.
The headphones block the noise: “that” performs the action.
3. The meal I made from the recipe which you sent me was surprisingly filling.
You sent the recipe: “which” is the object.
4. I kept the box that contains the warranty documents.
The box contains the documents: “that” is the subject.

Productive speaking

Describe one possession in layers

Choose an object that you have owned for at least a few years. Build your answer in four layers:

  1. Identify it precisely: size, shape, material, quality or colour.
  2. Say where it came from and who gave or sold it to you.
  3. Add one defining and one non-defining relative clause.
  4. Explain why you keep it, even if it is no longer valuable or fashionable.
Show a complete model after Sergey has answered

I still use a sturdy rectangular desk that I bought when I moved into my first flat. The carpenter who made it worked in a small local workshop. The desk, which is now covered in scratches, has moved with me three times. It is not priceless, but I keep it because it is practical and reminds me of an important period in my life.
