Changing opinions

When we return to a book after many years, our reaction may change. Mind or opinion?

change your mind — change a decision or a plan.

change your opinion of something / someone — start to judge it differently.

1I planned to read the long novel, but I changed my ___ and chose a short story instead.
Did the plan change, or the judgement?
mind
2I loved this novel at 17, but my ___ of it has changed over the years.
Did the plan change, or the judgement?
opinion
3After rereading the book, I changed my ___ of the main character: now he seems selfish to me.
Look at the word after the gap.
opinion
4I was going to give the book to my sister, but I changed my ___ and kept it.
Did the plan change, or the judgement?
mind

When a famous painting was first shown, critics hated it. Today people queue to see it. Suggest ONE reason why opinions changed.

04 · 12 min

A second reading

A B1+ text written for this lesson.

The same story, a different reader

Last spring, Anna took an old science-fiction novel off her bookshelf. She had first read it at nineteen, on a long train journey, and had finished it in two days. Back then, she was mainly interested in the plot: a small crew travelling to a distant planet, and a captain who never seemed to make a mistake.

Reading it again twenty-five years later was a surprisingly different experience. The plot hadn't lost its power, but Anna kept noticing things she had completely missed before. The captain, whom she had once admired, now struck her as rather selfish: several of his “brave” decisions put the rest of the crew at risk. Meanwhile, a quiet engineer she had barely remembered turned out to be the most interesting character in the book.

“The book hasn't changed. I have,” she told her book club. Not everyone agreed. One member argued that a really good novel shouldn't depend on the reader's age at all.

Anna still recommends the book, but with a warning: the first eighty pages are slow, and readers who expect non-stop action may give up too early. Those who keep going, she says, will find it far more thought-provoking than it first appears.

1How long did it take Anna to finish the novel the first time?
Paragraph 1.
two days
2How does the captain seem to Anna now? Write ONE adjective.
Paragraph 2. struck her as = seemed to her.
selfish
3Which character does she now find the most interesting? Write ONE word.
Paragraph 2, last sentence.
the engineer
4Did everyone in the book club agree with Anna? Yes or no?
Paragraph 3.
no
5Which readers may give up too early? Readers who expect ___.
Paragraph 4. Two words.
non-stop action

Clear and confident

Polite does not mean apologising for everything. A clear request is polite.

I'd like… · Could you…? · Is it possible to…? · I need…

1Too direct: “Give me my money back.” → Clear and polite: “I'd like ___ for these tickets, please.”
Two words: article + noun.
a refund
2The hotel sent me the transfer details. I'll ___ their email to you, so you can see the times.
To pass on an email you received.
forward (also: send)
3Could you send me the new schedule ___ email, please?
A preposition, like ___ phone, ___ post.
by (also: via)
4My room is next to the lift and it's very noisy. Could I ___ to another floor?
Change to a different room.
move

You booked a place at a book club meeting, but the date has changed to Thursday, and you work late on Thursdays. Ask the organiser to move you to the next meeting and give ONE reason. No “I'm sorry”.
