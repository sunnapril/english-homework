2. Ability across time

Can, could and be able to describe the ability or opportunity to do something.

CAN / CAN’T · PRESENT

General ability: I can speak French.
Ability at a particular time: I can’t open the window.

COULD / COULDN’T · PAST

General past ability: I could swim when I was six.
Particular negative result: I couldn’t open the door.

WAS / WERE ABLE TO

An alternative to can/could. For one positive completed action in the past: I was able to fix it in the end.

BE ABLE TO · OTHER FORMS

I’ll be able to… · I’ve been able to… · I’d like to be able to… · I enjoy being able to…

Register: be able to can replace can/could and is often slightly more formal. It is essential when the grammar needs will, the present perfect, an infinitive or an -ing form.

9. What would be worth learning?

Choose three questions. Answer as yourself; the language support is optional.

1. What practical problem at home would you like to be able to solve without calling anyone?

2. What could you do confidently as a child but would struggle to do now?

3. If you had one free evening every week, what would you learn — and what would you refuse to spend time on?

4. Which is more useful in real life: being able to cook five good meals, put up shelves or speak another language?

5. Have you ever been able to learn something difficult faster than you expected? What helped?
