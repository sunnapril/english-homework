2. One request, five useful frames

The longer frames often sound more careful, but the situation and tone matter more than a fixed “politeness level”.

Could you + base verb

Could you collect my parcel?

Would you mind + verb-ing

Would you mind collecting my parcel?

Do you think you could + base verb

Do you think you could collect it?

One more request

Just one more thing, if it wouldn’t be too much trouble.

Would it be possible for you + to-infinitive

Would it be possible for you to collect it?

a common trap

3. Would you mind…?

You ask whether the action would bother the other person. That is why a natural acceptance is often negative.

Request

Would you mind waiting five minutes while I finish this call?

You agree to wait

No, not at all.
Of course not.

Notice

Yes may sound as if waiting is a problem. A longer clear answer is safest: No problem — I can wait.

keep it natural

4. Accept, hesitate or refuse

ACCEPT

Sure, no problem.
Yes, I can do that.

CHECK FIRST

It depends what it is.
When do you need it?

REFUSE POLITELY

I’m afraid I can’t.
I’m really sorry, but…

REACT

Never mind.
Thanks anyway.

7. Real favours, real details

Choose two situations. Make the request, then answer as yourself.

• You are travelling for a week. Ask a friend to water your plants and check your flat once.

• A parcel has arrived while you are at work. Ask someone at home to accept it.

• Your phone is almost dead before an evening badminton game. Ask a colleague for a charger.

• You cannot leave work before a deadline. Ask a colleague who is going out to bring you something for lunch.

Say why · give the time or place · make it easy to refuse.
Do you think you could …? · Would you mind …-ing? · It’s not a problem if … · I’m afraid …
