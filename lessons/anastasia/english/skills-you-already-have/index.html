5Precise contrast

Same or equal?

the same
identical, or not different
We chose the same target.
They started at the same age.
equal
the same in number, amount, value, size or rights
The two teams have an equal number of points.
Men and women should have equal opportunities.
Important: use the same, but normally no the before equal + noun: the same age, but equal opportunities.
We went to ___ primary school.
Both riders completed an ___ number of rounds.
These two pistols aren’t identical, but they are ___ in weight.
Make two true sentences: one with the same and one with equal.

6Natural English

Have a great time or spend time?

have a great time
enjoy an experience
We had a great time at the riding centre.
spend time
say how or where you use your time
We spent an hour practising.
I spent time with my friends.
We spent time well is grammatically possible, but it is not the natural choice for «мы отлично провели время». Say We had a great time. For productive use of time, say We used our time well or It was time well spent.
The lesson was fun. We ___.
We ___ nearly an hour at the shooting range.
We finished everything important. We ___.
6One verb, two ideas

Prepare carefully or be well-prepared?

I prepared carefully for the competition.
prepared = an action in the past
How did I prepare? Carefully.
I am well-prepared for the competition.
am well-prepared = my state now
I am ready because I prepared.
I am prepared carefully does not express this idea naturally. It sounds passive, as if another person carefully prepares you. For your own state, use I am well-prepared.
I ___ carefully for my first shooting competition.
The interview starts in ten minutes, but I’m not nervous. I ___.
She wants to hit the target, so she needs to ___.
8Useful verb patterns

Might, finish and verb + to + verb

might + base verb
something is possible
I might start horse riding again.
She might hit the target.
verb + to + verb
want to learn · need to practise · decide to try · forget to call
The second verb stays in the base form after to.
Finish is usually a verb: finish work, finish a race. It can also be a noun, especially for the end of a race: the start and the finish. It can also describe a surface: a smooth finish.
Question word + to + verb: I don’t know what to do first.
Also: She knows how to prepare. We decided where to meet.
I’m not sure, but I ___ go to the shooting range next week.
She decided ___ horse riding again.
I have three urgent tasks and I don’t know ___.
The runners could see the ___ at the end of the road.
I need to ___ this report before the court session.
