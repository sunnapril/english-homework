6 · What is the problem?

Listen and match dishes 2–6 with problems a–f. Salad is the example.

Show audio script
Ken: I’ve got a text from Dad. Lily: Oh, yes? What about? Ken: He and Mum aren’t going to be home until six. He asked us to make dinner. Lily: Oh, OK. We can do that, right? Ken: I guess so. Um, how about making a salad? That’s easy to do. Lily: Good idea. Let me check what we’ve got … OK, so it looks like we’ve got a few tomatoes, a cucumber and some mushrooms, but no lettuce, carrots, celery or oil. And Dad likes onions in salad, but we haven’t got any of those. Ken: OK, so no to salad. How about hamburgers? We all love those. Lily: True, but Mum isn’t eating meat at the moment, so I don’t think she’d be happy with a burger … Um, what about making some soup? Ken: Just soup? That doesn’t sound like a proper meal. I think we’d all feel hungry afterwards. Lily: You’re right. Um, well, what about making a fish pie? That’s Dad’s favourite. Ken: Good idea, but won’t fish pie take ages to prepare? Mum and Dad are getting home at six, so we need something that takes 50 minutes or less … Oh, I know! Let’s make some pizzas. There are always some pizza bases in the freezer, and we’ve got tomatoes, mushrooms and lots of cheese. Lily: But don’t you remember? We had pizzas for dinner yesterday! Ken: So? Pizzas are always good … No, you’re right. We should have something different. Lily: An omelette? Ken: Good idea … but Dad’s text said not to use the eggs because he wants to use them tomorrow. Lily: Uh, we could … I’ve got it! Let’s make pasta. I can use the tomatoes to make a sauce. Ken: And we can add the mushrooms to the sauce as well. Great idea! Let’s get started.

7 · Maggie’s habits

Listen and choose an adverb of frequency. Sentence 1 is the example.

Show audio script
Presenter: Today we’re lucky to have Maggie Peckham on the show. Maggie runs Wish List, the popular internet shopping site … Now, your business earns millions of pounds a year, right Maggie? Maggie: I don’t really focus on money, but yeah, I think so. Presenter: So what are some reasons for your success? Maggie: Every successful person I know has been lucky. So good luck is definitely one reason. Presenter: OK, but luck happens to you, right? I mean, it’s not something you do. Are there any things you do that help you to succeed? Maggie: Um … I guess there are a few things I do. I think it’s very important to balance working and relaxing. I usually take a short holiday every month … and I believe that if you eat bad food, you can’t work well, so I always try to eat healthily. Presenter: Mm, anything else? Maggie: Well, it’s a good idea to change things in your life sometimes. For example, instead of driving to work every day, take the bus or train sometimes … and if you sleep well, you can make good decisions, I believe. So I rarely stay up past eleven. Presenter: Uh-huh. Maggie: I like reading, too. I find that I often get new ideas from books … and, err …, I never get angry. Why? Well, I find that when I’m angry, I make bad choices. Presenter: Thanks, Maggie. Now, you said it’s sometimes good to change things in your life. Could you say more? Maggie: Sure, OK … when you change something about your life, you might see something new, or meet somebody new. And those can help you come up with new ideas. Presenter: Mm, for example? Maggie: Well, I often go to Berlin for my work, OK? I usually fly, but one day, about three years ago, I decided to take the train. Presenter: A train from London to Berlin?! How long does that take? Maggie: About ten hours, so I had a lot of time just to sit and relax … which is unusual because usually I’m very busy. Anyway, I started chatting to the person opposite me. He used Wish List often and had some great ideas about how to improve the site. In fact, by the end of the journey, I gave him a job! He’s now one of my senior managers. So, because I changed something and took the train to Berlin instead of the plane, my company became more successful. Presenter: Wow! I see: a small change really did make a big difference …

1. Maggie usually takes a short holiday each month. — example

2. She tries to eat food that is good for her.
3. She thinks it is a good idea to change things about your life.
4. She goes to bed later than 11 p.m.
5. She can think of new ideas from books.
6. She gets angry because she makes bad choices when she feels that way.
