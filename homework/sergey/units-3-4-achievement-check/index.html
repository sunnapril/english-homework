1 · Achievement Test 2 · Listening Ex.1

Childhood memories

Listen to someone talking about childhood memories. Decide whether each statement is True or False.

So I guess my very earliest childhood memory was from when I was about two and I got stung by a bee. I’d been crawling around on the grass we had in the back garden and I must have put my hand on the bee, because the next thing I knew I was in a lot of pain and my mum was picking me up. I don’t know what she did, though. I don’t think we went to the hospital or anything like that. In fact, I’m not really sure if I remember this story or if I’ve just imagined it after my mum told me about it! Your memories are a bit unpredictable, I think. One memorable event was my first day at school. I really hadn’t wanted to go, but Mum dropped me off at the school gates and in I went. When the bell sounded for lunchtime, I walked out the school gates and the two kilometres down the road back home. I opened the door and said ‘Hi Mum. I’ve been to school!’ feeling happy to have got through the first day. Of course my mum looked at me in shock. Then she put me in the car and took me back for the afternoon classes! Another childhood event I recall is breaking my arm. Me and my two brothers were very competitive and always chasing each other around the house. The things we got up to were so dangerous it’s remarkable one of us didn’t get seriously injured. I’ll never forget one time when my older brother ran through a glass door – he didn’t realise it had been closed – but then picked himself up from the pile of broken glass without a scratch. I really don’t know how our mother was able to cope with us when we were young. Anyway, when the weather was good enough – and maybe sometimes when it wasn’t – Mum used to send us outside, either to the garden or into the street, so that we could run around and use up some energy. But on this particular day, the weather must have been very bad, because we were inside. We had been playing our ‘hide and seek’ game for a while and I was hiding on the top level of a two-level bunk bed. Quite soon, my younger brother found me and I tried to escape by jumping off the bed. As I started to jump, my brother grabbed my ankles and I fell down onto the floor and landed on my arm. Ouch! It was incredibly painful, but I was stubborn and determined not to tell Mum what had happened. I spent the rest of the day hoping my arm would feel better, but the longer it went on the more painful it became. Eventually, at dinner time, Mum noticed that I was only using one hand to eat and the story came out. She got me to the hospital as fast as she could and I came home with a big white plaster around my arm, trying not to look too proud.
2 · Achievement Test 2 · Listening Ex.2–3

Possessions with stories

First match speakers 1–6 with descriptions a–f. Then listen again and choose the correct detail for each speaker.

P: Welcome to 20-second Talk In, the programme where you, our listeners, get to talk about a topic for twenty seconds. Today’s topic? Possessions. Do you have a particular possession that is special to you, or which has a story? If so, give us a call. And here’s our first caller, Tomas. Hello! Tomas: Hi. So, I want to talk about a cheap, leather wallet I have. It's lucky, because I’ve lost it several times, but it keeps coming back to me. Once I left it on a train in another country, but a very honest person found it and sent it to me by post. Luckily, there had been something inside with my home address on it. Another time, it fell out of my pocket when I was cycling to work. I didn’t even realise I’d lost it until I was going home and saw it in the street! It was also stolen once – the thief took the money, but left everything else and threw the wallet in a field. Later, someone walking their dog saw it and managed to contact me via the library – my library card was in there. So, … P: Thanks, Tomas! Sorry to cut you off, but you’ve only got 20 seconds, remember! Next up is Bella. Go ahead, Bella. Bella: Well, my favourite possession is a little different, as it’s living. It’s a rose plant which has been in my family for a really long time. Each generation cuts off one of the young branches and grows it in a pot to pass on to the next generation. It doesn’t have a great smell, but it’s a beautiful orange colour. I really love it. I’ve just given a new plant to my son and daughter-in-law so they can grow the rose, too. P: Thank you, Bella. Fascinating. Now, our next caller is Tanya. Tanya: Hi, yes. I want to tell you about something very special to me. It’s a necklace with a long silver chain and a piece of glass shaped like a sheep. My sister bought it for me because I love sheep. When I go walking in the countryside I spend hours just watching them. I find them very peaceful. Anyway, I don’t wear the necklace, but it hangs in my kitchen window so that when the sun shines the glass makes nice colours on the wall. P: Very nice, Tanya. Thanks. Who’s next? David? David: Yes, it is. Well, I’m going to be short and sweet. My favourite thing is an old patterned, red shirt. It’s got holes in it and my wife wants me to throw it away. But I’ve had it for years and I can’t get rid of it – it’s a part of me! P: OK, short and sweet indeed, David. Hello, Rachel? Rachel: Yes, hi. Well, ever since I was a child I’ve always wanted to visit Japan. I think it was an old Japanese film that first got my attention. Finally, last year, I went there for two weeks and I really had a wonderful time. I bought a lot of souvenirs, but the thing which I love most – which really is my favourite possession now – is this elegant, rectangular box. It’s shiny black with a simple, decorative pattern. It wasn’t very expensive, but it’s priceless to me. P: Thanks for sharing that story, Rachel. And finally to Simon. Hi? Simon: Well, this possession I don’t have anymore, but it’s something that I’ll never forget. It is – or was, I suppose – a large, designer watch. I never took it off, so forgot to remove it when I went swimming in the sea and it slipped off my wrist. I spent hours looking for it, but … Well, I guess I’ll have to save up and buy another one. P: Ah, sorry to hear that. Thanks, Simon, and to all our callers …
