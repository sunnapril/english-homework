2 · Workbook p.31 · exercises 3 and 4

A digital detox

Source: Roadmap B2 Workbook, Unit 4, Recording 1. Listen once for the conversation and again for details. For the short-answer questions, write no more than four words for each answer.

Full word-for-word script — open after the second listen

Sophie: Hey, James, have you seen this meme that Lewis has shared?
James: Yeah, it’s really funny!
Mara: I haven’t. I’m on a digital detox at the moment.
Sophie: A digital what?
Mara: Detox … it means going offline for a month.
Sophie: Oh, Mara! Why?
Mara: I don’t know. I just waste so much time looking at pictures of other people’s pets and babies. Babies I’ve never even met!
James: But what about work?
Mara: Yeah, I use the internet at work, obviously. I’m talking about when I’m at home.
Sophie: Interesting … since when?
Mara: Last Thursday.
Sophie: But you said you spent all weekend binge-watching a police drama!
Mara: Oh, yeah. OK, so it’s just social media that I’m avoiding, not TV shows.
James: I don’t think I could cope without social media! I’d feel like I was missing out.
Mara: I thought I would too but it turns out I quite like it. It’s nice to be able to focus on something without getting distracted by messages. I’ve even started reading a book. First one in ages!
James: I know what you mean about the distractions. It can be hard to concentrate on something when your phone keeps buzzing. I can’t stop myself looking though.
Mara: Well, our phones are designed that way, aren’t they?
James: What do you mean?
Mara: Well, when we get a message, our body releases a chemical into our brains. It’s called dopamine, I think. It makes us feel good. Designers make our phones buzz when a message arrives so we feel good and keep using our phone.
James: That sounds scary – like an addiction.
Mara: Well, it is in a way. Designers want us to form habits so we use their apps. It’s why they include features like those three little dots that appear when someone’s writing us a message.
Sophie: How so?
Mara: They promise us some kind of reward – a message – and just the thought of that causes our brains to produce dopamine.
James: Wow! Now I know why I spend so much time staring at those dots!
Mara: Ha ha! Exactly! And while we wait, we feel both stressed and excited about what the message will be.
Sophie: So, if we get a lift when we receive a message, does that mean we feel low when we don’t get one?
Mara: Hmm, good question. I have no idea of the science but I know from experience that it’s disappointing when the three dots appear and then disappear.
Sophie: You’re right!
Mara: I did read about an experiment where two groups of university students were asked to attend a lecture. One group had their phones in their bags. The other group left their phones and bags in another room. After a while, the researchers abandoned the experiment because the group without their phones were getting so anxious, the researchers thought it was endangering their mental well-being!
Sophie: You’re joking. I mean, I love my social media, but that’s a bit extreme! I think I could manage an hour or two without my phone.
Mara: It’s called separation anxiety. It’s something more and more of us are suffering from.
Sophie: Are we really so desperate to keep in touch with people?
Mara: Maybe. But some scientists think it’s because our phones hold our digital memories, so if we lose our phone, it’s like losing our own memory. That’s what worries us.
Sophie: Oh right, that kind of makes sense.
James: I did read about a new phobia – nomophobia. It’s the fear of being without your phone. I guess it’s the same as separation anxiety.
Sophie: Well, I don’t think I suffer from either and I want to prove it. I’m willing to go on a digital detox. James, do you want to give it a try?
James: No way. I’ll support you though if you like!
