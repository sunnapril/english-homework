1 · Neither, either, both
1. ___ option solves the whole problem.
Not the first and not the second.
2. I don't fully agree with ___ suggestion.
A negative verb is already present.
3. ___ colleagues need to revise their plans.
The plural noun refers to the two colleagues together.
2 · Precision
4. The discussion created ___ pressure.
Pressure is strong in degree.
5. The company organised an ___ training week.
The training is concentrated into a short period.
6. Delaying the launch was the most ___ decision.
The decision was reasonable.
7. Performance reviews can be a ___ subject.
The subject needs careful treatment.
3 · Do in spoken English
It should do and That should do usually mean the same thing: “it/that should be enough or suitable”. Use it for something already identified; use that when pointing to what was just said, done or added.

“Will this cable work?” — “It should do.”
“I’ve added one more example.” — “Good. That should do.”
8. “Will Friday do?” = Is Friday ___?
The speaker is asking whether the date works.
9. We have enough examples now. “___.”
Both That should do and It should do are natural here.
10. I'm exhausted. I ___ a break.
This spoken phrase means would like or need.
